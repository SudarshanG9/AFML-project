# 2. Literature review and the limits of novelty

## How the review was assembled

The supplied Elicit critical assessment and eight-row closest-papers CSV were read as secondary research aids. They identify methodological overlaps; they are not experiments and their quoted passages do not establish that the team reproduced a paper. Primary papers, official proceedings, author repositories and institutional records were checked where accessible on 15 November 2026. The exact supplied inputs and unavailable sources are indexed in [Chapter 10](10_EVIDENCE_AND_REFERENCES.md).

Several details needed reconciliation. The assessment labels Sensors-Driven as conference work, while the inspected publisher identifies a Sensors article. The CSV's AV-DeepFake-Net DOI differs from the institutional record; this chapter links the named work directly instead of silently combining those identifiers. The proposal's shorthand “Smeu et al.” and the distinct DeepFake Doctor paper refer to related but different shortcut studies. No original Undermind response was located. Its supposed conclusions are unverified and play no evidential role here. A separate pretrained-model assessment and the two older PDFs cited by Elicit were also not supplied as standalone files; their reported content remains secondary.

## Joint audiovisual representation learning

**AVFF.** Oorloff and colleagues address detectors that learn superficial training-set correlations instead of useful audiovisual correspondence. Their two-stage method first learns on real videos with complementary modality masking, contrastive learning and reconstruction, then learns supervised real/fake classification. This established that jointly learned cross-modal representations are a substantive detection strategy before AVFuse-CausalQ. It made AVFF an attractive candidate architecture, but the paper's success did not supply our team with a verified, runnable checkpoint at the feasibility gate. Its study also does not answer how rounding Q/K/V changes this project's selected detector. Dependence on coherent audiovisual inputs remains a deployment limitation. We cite the primary [AVFF paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02951v1), rather than adopting the proposal's checkpoint-search result as a current claim that no implementation exists anywhere.

**DiMoDif.** Koutlis and Papadopoulos use speech-related pretrained representations and a modality-information differentiation module to detect differences between what the two streams convey. Their framework supports detection and temporal localization, with joint transformer processing and feature-pyramid information. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10193v2) and [official implementation](https://github.com/mever-team/dimodif) made a frozen pretrained detection path available for this investigation. AVFuse-CausalQ uses that path and its learned head; it does not reproduce the training or claim the upstream localization results as its own. The inspectable joint self-attention supplied a concrete precision boundary. The upstream paper's broad robustness and localization investigations should not be confused with our single-checkpoint, single-cohort activation experiment.

**AuViRe.** This related reconstruction approach predicts speech-related modality representations and uses reconstruction discrepancies for temporal forgery localization. It offers a different way to expose disagreements than an attention-only perturbation. The supplied Elicit sources discuss it as a candidate conceptual comparator, and the [primary paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18993) describes the method rather than validating a checkpoint for our experiment. We did not integrate or benchmark AuViRe. Its reconstruction/localization objective helps explain why general cross-modal reasoning is established prior art, while leaving the precision question for the implemented DiMoDif path unanswered. Detailed real-world performance claims in the secondary review are not used as comparative numbers here.

**Explicit Correlation Learning.** Cai and colleagues address overreliance on audiovisual synchronization by distilling content-level correlations from audio and visual speech recognizers. Their [ICME paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19171) shows why a detector should learn more than a timing mismatch heuristic. Its contribution motivates separating audio-only, video-only and joint fakes and avoiding claims that synchronization alone explains detection. It does not examine this study's fake-INT8 attention boundaries. AVFuse-CausalQ's aggregate and silence-control results also do not demonstrate that it preserved the particular content correlations learned by that method; that would require dedicated probes.

## Dataset papers define what a score can mean

**FakeAVCeleb.** Khalid and colleagues introduced a multimodal dataset with authentic and generated audio/video combinations, addressing the limitations of datasets focused on facial manipulation alone. Its [NeurIPS dataset paper](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/d9d4f495e875a2e075a1a4a6e1b9770f-Abstract-round2.html) supports the four-way manipulation taxonomy that shaped the proposal. Access conditions and artifacts remain important: a dataset's native category labels do not by themselves ensure source separation or eliminate audio shortcuts. The project ultimately did not evaluate on FakeAVCeleb, so it cannot claim a transfer result on that dataset.

**LAV-DF.** Cai and colleagues construct content-driven localized audiovisual manipulations and provide temporal annotations. This makes the difference between a whole-video label and a short evaluation window consequential: an edited word may be outside the window. The [official dataset/paper repository](https://github.com/ControlNet/LAV-DF) supports the selected benchmark and its terms. Our final task is whole-video classification, not temporal localization, and our selected 3,200 videos are a subset of the dataset. Access to a compatible pretrained checkpoint and explicit audio/video manipulation flags made it practical, but this choice narrows the external validity of the result.

## Efficient attention is established systems work

**FlashAttention.** Dao and colleagues identify memory traffic as a central attention cost and use tiling plus online softmax to avoid storing the full score matrix in high-bandwidth memory. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135) establishes exact IO-aware attention as prior art. It motivates inspecting actual shapes and choosing an existing implementation before building another kernel. It does not imply every SDPA call on every device uses FlashAttention, nor that a theoretical dense matrix equals measured peak allocation. AVFuse-CausalQ's CPU timings and dense oracle are a different measurement scope.

**SageAttention.** Zhang and colleagues study accurate low-bit attention and provide a plug-in acceleration method using carefully designed quantization and execution. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02367) establishes that quantized attention can be a real systems technique rather than merely a numerical simulation. Its kernels, granularity and smoothing choices differ from the simple global per-tensor round/dequantize function used here. Its performance results on other models do not predict forensic sensitivity in our checkpoint. Conversely, our fake-quant latency cannot refute or reproduce SageAttention's hardware acceleration.

**QFlash.** Oh, Kwon and Lee combine quantization with fused attention, including integer-domain softmax and scale-management machinery, in a Triton implementation. The [IJCAI paper](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/167) and [official code](https://github.com/EfficientCompLab/qflash) directly rule out novelty in “integer-only memory-efficient attention.” This study never implements that kernel: it measures rounded activations under floating arithmetic. Another 2026 FPGA work also uses the QFlash name; it is a separate design, not the Oh/Kwon/Lee paper named by our proposal. Treating fake quantization as equivalent to either system would obscure both correctness and cost.

## Deepfake-specific quantization and efficiency overlap

**DeFakeQ.** Li and colleagues address loss of subtle forensic features under compression. Their method combines horizontal adaptive quantization across model blocks with vertical feature refinement/restoration, using a task-aware adaptation process. The [primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08847v1) provides direct precedent for nonuniform precision and selective full-precision information in deepfake detectors. It principally studies visual detector settings rather than our fixed joint audiovisual attention intervention. This difference supports a narrower experimental question; it does not permit claiming selective restoration as a new idea. AVFuse-CausalQ does not implement HAQ or VEFT, does not retrain with their objective, and cannot present its four static modes as a reproduction of DeFakeQ.

**Lightweight quantized image detectors.** Tao and colleagues propose connected quantized blocks and a shifted logarithmic activation quantizer to retain forensic features in low-bit image models. Their [IJCAI paper](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/59) establishes another direct forensic quantization precedent. Full-precision shortcuts and task-specific quantizers differ from globally rounding four attention boundaries in a frozen joint model. Operation/storage reductions in such work should also be distinguished from measured end-to-end device latency. It prevents a broad “first low-bit deepfake detector” claim without settling our modality-stratified experiment.

**AuthentiQ.** The supplied CSV identifies Iyengar, Mallampalli and Sanghvi's on-device PTQ/QAT and NPU work, associated with VideoMAE and AST. The [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/MIPR70517.2026.00090) was not accessible for primary full-text verification in this audit. The secondary assessment says the audio and video models are evaluated separately rather than through a jointly fused detector. That distinction is provisionally relevant, but must not be upgraded into a verified criticism of the original implementation. We include AuthentiQ as a deployment/quantization overlap with restricted verification, use none of its numerical speed claims as a baseline, and make no claim that it lacks every form of joint processing.

**AV-DeepFake-Net.** Saleem and colleagues combine visual/audio encoders, temporal boundary modeling, cross-modal attention and uncertainty-based modality weighting. The [institutional publication record](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/output/5235118/av-deepfake-net-attention-guided-and-uncertainty-aware-network-for-audiovisual-deepfake-detection) supports the architectural and localization overlap. Entropy-based trust in a modality is conceptually different from choosing numerical precision, but both concern reliable use of audiovisual evidence. The supplied review questions the detail behind its quantized-cost claims; we do not present that secondary concern as a reproduced failure. Neither its reported accuracy nor its speed can be directly ranked against our selected LAV-DF cohort and CPU cached-feature boundary.

**Sensors-Driven multimodal detection.** Waseem and colleagues combine Res2Net audio, temporal visual features, bidirectional cross-attention and modality gates, with an edge-oriented implementation. The [publisher article](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/12/3695) makes joint AV fusion plus efficient deployment an existing research direction. Learned reliability gates adapt how evidence is combined; our modes instead hold model parameters fixed and perturb selected arithmetic boundaries. The study supplies no matched reproduction of that model's dataset protocol or edge runtime. The review's concerns about quantization detail motivate cautious comparison rather than categorical claims about superiority.

## Shortcuts and interpretation

Smeu and colleagues' [CVPR shortcut study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00175) exposes leading-silence signals that can let detectors perform well for the wrong reason, and investigates learning from authentic data. Klemt, Segna and Rohrbach's separate [DeepFake Doctor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05851) examines datasets, methods and protocols, introducing SIMBA and revised benchmark analysis. Together they show why a precision-stable shortcut detector is not necessarily precision-stable forensic reasoning. Our retained control matches trailing silence on LAV-DF; it is a related validity check, not a reproduction of leading-silence removal or proof that all artifacts are controlled.

Jain and Wallace's [attention interpretation study](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1357/) questions reading attention weights directly as explanations. This motivates label-grounded outcomes here. It does not establish that attention can never support an explanation in any setting; the relevant lesson is to test the relationship rather than infer correctness from a visually similar attention map.

## Comparison and the contribution actually investigated

| Prior-art family | Already established | What this experiment adds | What remains outside our evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AVFF, DiMoDif, AuViRe | learned joint AV representations and forensic detection/localization | instrument an available frozen DiMoDif detector | new detector, training improvement or localization benchmark |
| FakeAVCeleb, LAV-DF | manipulation categories and labeled benchmarks | fixed whole-video cohort and source-aware comparison | new dataset or cross-dataset generalization |
| FlashAttention | IO-aware exact tiled attention | measured-shape scope decision and dense/SDPA checks | new kernel or measured memory reduction |
| SageAttention, QFlash | low-bit attention execution and kernel optimization | task-grounded sensitivity to a simpler simulated precision axis | native INT8 acceleration |
| DeFakeQ, Tao et al. | forensic-aware nonuniform/low-bit quantization | static V/output versus Q interventions in joint attention | first restoration method or superiority to their algorithms |
| AuthentiQ, AV-DeepFake-Net, Sensors-Driven | relevant edge, multimodal and quantization overlap, with varying primary access | explicit arithmetic boundaries, uncertainty and measurement scope | first edge AV detector or matched deployment win |
| Correlation/shortcut studies | synchrony and artifacts can mislead detectors | manipulation strata and a retained silence control | comprehensive causal explanation or shortcut immunity |

The provisional contribution is therefore a traceable, label-grounded comparison of four precision configurations inside five joint self-attention layers of one frozen detector, with source-grouped uncertainty and separately scoped CPU runtime evidence. The review supports the value of that particular experiment. It does not prove global absence of prior work, equal-budget superiority, or an architectural invention. The observed answer is developed in the results chapters without converting a proposed literature gap into a demonstrated optimization success.
